- **Stream C:** Market-origin, focused on price discovery, which requires awareness of both states for global index pricing.

**Architectural Diagnosis:** The Houston Node is not malfunctioning. Rather, it operates correctly within a broken jurisdictional paradigm. ## What the Reconciliation Layer Does


The Reconciliation Layer serves a critical function in systems where multiple, potentially contradictory truths must coexist and interact. Its operation is defined by four core capabilities, each designed to maintain stability and enable interoperability in an environment shaped by jurisdictional divergence and regulatory fragmentation.


### Key Functions of the Reconciliation Layer


1. **Truth Encapsulation**


Each jurisdiction’s version of truth is secured within a cryptographic “truth capsule.” This ensures that the internal logic and regulatory requirements of a given region are preserved, without demanding universal acceptance or consensus from all systems. The encapsulation allows for the coexistence of differing truths across domains and prevents the imposition of one standard over another.


1. **Cross‑Truth Routing**


The Reconciliation Layer intelligently determines which downstream systems are permitted to access and process specific truth capsules. For example, USD financial rails may be restricted to only accept “Sanctioned” capsules, while RMB rails may only process “Settled” capsules. Global indices, such as those used for energy markets, can ingest both types of capsules but apply different weighting or logic as appropriate. This routing ensures that regulatory boundaries are respected and that each system only interacts with compatible truth states.


1. USD rails → Only “Sanctioned” capsule
2. RMB rails → Only “Settled” capsule
3. Global energy index → Both capsules, but weighted differently
4. **Temporal Harmonization**


Truths often diverge not only in substance but also in timing. The Reconciliation Layer organizes and aligns these asynchronous updates into a single, coherent timeline. This temporal harmonization is critical to ensure that all systems have a synchronized understanding of events, reducing confusion and preventing errors that might arise from out-of-sync data streams.


1. **Contradiction Buffering**


When conflicting truths arise—such as a regulatory update from one authority (e.g., OFAC) clashing with the settlement finality declared by another (e.g., PBOC)—the Reconciliation Layer institutes a buffer window. During this period, both states are recognized as valid, allowing time for higher-order rules or authorities to resolve the discrepancy. This process acts as a safeguard, protecting the overall system from shocks or cascading failures triggered by abrupt contradictions.


By performing these functions, the Reconciliation Layer prevents systemic shock and maintains the integrity of interconnected systems, even as they operate under different and occasionally conflicting regimes.
