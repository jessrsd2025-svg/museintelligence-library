- The Silk Road’s origins trace back to ancient times. Even before the formal establishment of the Silk Road, the **Royal Road** existed. This road connected **Susa (in present-day Iran)** to **Sardis (near the Mediterranean Sea in modern Turkey)** during the **Achaemenid Empire**—approximately **300 years before the Silk Road**.
- The Persians expanded the Royal Road to include smaller routes connecting **Mesopotamia to the Indian subcontinent** and **northern Africa via Egypt**. These routes eventually became part of the broader Silk Road.


1. **Silk Road History and Routes:**
  - The east-west trade routes between **Greece and China** began opening during the **first and second centuries B.C.**
  - The **Roman Empire** and the **Kushan Empire** (which ruled northern India) also benefited from Silk Road commerce.
  - Interestingly, the ancient Greek word for China is **“Seres,”** which literally means **“the land of silk.”** However, the term **“Silk Road”** wasn’t coined until **1877** by German geographer Ferdinand von Richthofen. Historians now prefer the term **“Silk Routes”**, acknowledging that there was more than one thoroughfare.
2. **Silk Road Routes to China:**
  - The Silk Road included a vast network of strategically located trading posts, markets, and thoroughfares.
  - [One route extended from the **Greco-Roman metropolis of Antioch** across the **Syrian Desert via Palmyra** to **Ctesiphon** (the Parthian capital) and **Seleucia** on the **Tigris River** in modern-day Iraq](https://www.history.com/topics/ancient-middle-east/silk-road)[1](https://www.history.com/topics/ancient-middle-east/silk-road).
  - [Another route ran through **northern Pakistan**, over the **Hindu Kush mountains**, into **Afghanistan**, and then followed a nearly straight line west through **mountainous northern Iran** and the **northern tip of the Syrian Desert** to the **Levant**](https://www.history.com/topics/ancient-middle-east/silk-road)[2](https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Silk_Road).


The Silk Road was a complex network of trade routes that connected distant civilizations, fostering cultural exchange and shaping history for centuries.

The route from the Taklamakan Desert to Syria would have historically been part of the Silk Road, the ancient network of trade routes that connected China with the Mediterranean. [The cities you mentioned, Kashgar, Aksu, Kuqa, Korla, and Turpan, were indeed key oases along the Silk Road’s northern route skirting the Taklamakan Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_along_the_Silk_Road)[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_along_the_Silk_Road). [From these cities, the route would extend westward towards Central Asia and eventually reach Syria, which was an important terminus of the Silk Road in the West](https://academic.oup.com/book/9102/chapter/269335933).

- [**Tea, paper, and bronze products** from China; **fabrics, spices, semi-precious stones, dyes, and ivory** from India; **cotton** from Central Asia; and **furs, cattle, and honey** from Europe](https://www.history.com/news/silk-road-trade-goods)[2](https://www.chinahighlights.com/silkroad/what-was-traded-and-why.htm).
