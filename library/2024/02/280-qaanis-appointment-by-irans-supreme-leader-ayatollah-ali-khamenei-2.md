The Complex Dynamics of Iran’s Political Landscape: A Tale of Assassinations and Power Struggles


In Iran, the political system diverges significantly from those of many other countries. Unlike in most nations, where the head of state holds paramount importance, in Iran, Ayatollahs, major religious leaders, play a pivotal role in shaping the country’s destiny. While an Ayatollah’s primary position is religious, it also extends to a central role in politics, influencing state matters and even participating in the selection of state leaders. Currently, Ayatollah Khomeini holds the authority to appoint key state figures, exerting influence over presidential elections and eligibility criteria for candidates.


Against this backdrop, a covert war is unfolding involving the United States, Israel, and Iran—a proxy war characterized by conflicts taking place far from the nations’ own territories. In this ongoing struggle, individuals closely associated with Ayatollah Khomeini are being systematically assassinated. This complex geopolitical chess game revolves around two crucial policies for Iran: nuclear armament and expanding its sphere of control in the region.


The responsibility for executing these policies fell upon two influential figures, Mossen Fakhrizadeh and Qassem Suleimani. Suleimani, the commander of the Revolutionary Guard, and Fakhrizadeh, recognized as one of Khamenei’s closest confidants, played instrumental roles in implementing Iran’s nuclear ambitions and expanding its influence in the Middle East, respectively.


Suleimani, considered a legend among the Iranian people, met his demise in a drone attack on January 3rd, following a meeting with government officials in Iraq. In response, Iran declared a period of national mourning and issued a stern warning against the unchecked assassinations. However, the absence of retaliation seemingly emboldened the attackers, leading to the subsequent assassination of Fakhrizadeh.


The decision to target Fakhrizadeh was reportedly based on intelligence seized by Israel’s agency, Mossad, during a 2018 operation in Iran. Documents identified him as the key figure behind Iran’s nuclear program, making his elimination crucial for regional power dynamics. Israel, in its quest to prevent a nuclear-armed Iran, had already engaged in cyber-attacks and sabotage against Iranian nuclear facilities.


The assassinations of these figures, under constant protection, required highly sophisticated methods. Fakhrizadeh, an insider in Iran, enjoyed continuous protection from Iranian intelligence. The Mossad meticulously planned the operation for eight months, assembling a team of over 20 agents, including Israelis and Iranians, to monitor Fakhrizadeh’s movements.


The actual assassination occurred on a Friday as Fakhrizadeh traveled to his villa in eastern Tehran. A remote-controlled weapon, weighing a ton with integrated explosives, was deployed along his known route. The weapon was remotely fired by agents, resulting in Fakhrizadeh’s death by 13 bullets. The integrated bomb destroyed evidence, and the Mossad team swiftly fled the scene, leaving Iran without detection.


As prominent figures close to Ayatollah Khomeini continue to be targeted and eliminated, there is a growing sense of isolation around Khamenei in Iran. The question that looms large is whether these calculated assassinations will persist, further reshaping the intricate political landscape of the region.

Current operations in the Red Sea: The US and Allied Forces have been conducting operations in the Red Sea to counter the threat posed by the Houthis, a rebel group in Yemen that is backed by Iran. The Houthis have been launching attacks on civilian and commercial vessels in the strategic waterway, using drones and missiles supplied by Tehran. The US and Allied Forces have responded by targeting the Houthis’ missile and drone launch sites, as well as their command-and-control centers, with precision airstrikes. The aim of these operations is to deter further attacks and protect the freedom of navigation in the Red Sea, which is vital for global trade and energy security.
