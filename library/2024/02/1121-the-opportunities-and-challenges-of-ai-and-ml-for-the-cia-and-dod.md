The role of AI in national defense is multifaceted and increasingly critical. AI technologies are being integrated to enhance decision-making, improve the accuracy and speed of operations, and maintain a strategic advantage. The Department of Defense’s strategy emphasizes the adoption of AI to ensure U.S. [warfighters maintain decision superiority on the battlefield](https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3578219/dod-releases-ai-adoption-strategy/)[1](https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3578219/dod-releases-ai-adoption-strategy/).


The Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI) collaborates with other intelligence agencies by sharing information, standardizing collaboration processes, and leading coordination on intelligence sharing and foreign liaison issues. [This collaboration extends to various sectors, including international, military, domestic, and private, to support intelligence missions while protecting privacy and civil liberties](https://www.dni.gov/index.php/how-we-work/collaboration)


The Deputy Assistant Director of National Intelligence (ADNI) for Data & Partnerships, a role currently held by Marie Falkowski, is responsible for the development and execution of data strategies, standards, and policies. [This position also facilitates the integration of advanced technologies such as AI, machine learning, quantum computing, and cloud computing into the intelligence community](https://www.hstoday.us/industry/people-on-the-move/marie-falkowski-takes-on-new-role-at-dni/)[3](https://www.hstoday.us/industry/people-on-the-move/marie-falkowski-takes-on-new-role-at-dni/).


The Joint Artificial Intelligence Center (JAIC), which was integrated into the Chief Digital and Artificial Intelligence Office (CDAO) in February 2022, was led by LtGen Michael Groen as of October 2020. [The goals of JAIC include accelerating the delivery and adoption of AI within the Department of Defense to achieve mission impact at scale, solving large and complex problem sets that span multiple combat systems, and ensuring real-time access to ever-improving libraries of data sets and tools](https://www.ai.mil/blog_12_31_20-what_a_year_for_the_jaic.html)[4](https://www.ai.mil/blog_12_31_20-what_a_year_for_the_jaic.html)[5](https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/2418970/joint-artificial-intelligence-center-has-substantially-grown-to-aid-the-warfigh/).

Artificial Intelligence (AI) plays a significant role in national defense, with applications including:


- **Intelligent and autonomous vehicles**, such as drones and robotic ships.
- **Intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance (ISR)** capabilities.
- **Predictive maintenance and logistics** to optimize military operations.
- **Information, cyber, and electronic warfare** to defend and attack in the digital domain.
- **Simulation and training** for better preparedness.
- **Command and control (C2)** systems for enhanced decision-making.
- [**Automated target recognition** for efficient and accurate threat identification](https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3578219/dod-releases-ai-adoption-strategy/)[1](https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/3578219/dod-releases-ai-adoption-strategy/)[2](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/in-depth-research-reports/report/eye-to-eye-in-ai/).


The Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI) ensures the protection of privacy and civil liberties while sharing intelligence through:


- Implementing **best practices** and **policies** that focus on the protection of privacy, civil liberties, and civil rights.
- The **Office of Civil Liberties, Privacy, and Transparency (CLPT)**, which works to carry out the national security mission in a manner that respects these values.
- [**Executive orders** and **directives** that establish processes for individuals to seek redress regarding alleged violations concerning their data](https://www.odni.gov/index.php/newsroom/reports-publications/reports-publications-2022/3616-odni-report-on-best-practices-to-protect-privacy-civil-liberties-and-civil-rights-of-americans-of-chinese-descent-in-the-conduct-of-u-s-intelligence-activities-1692377383)[3](https://www.odni.gov/index.php/newsroom/reports-publications/reports-publications-2022/3616-odni-report-on-best-practices-to-protect-privacy-civil-liberties-and-civil-rights-of-americans-of-chinese-descent-in-the-conduct-of-u-s-intelligence-activities-1692377383)[4](https://www.dni.gov/index.php/who-we-are/organizations/197-about/organization/office-of-civil-liberties-privacy-and-transparency)[5](https://www.dni.gov/index.php/features/197-about/organization/office-of-civil-liberties-privacy-and-transparency)[6](https://www.odni.gov/index.php/ncsc-how-we-work/197-about/organization/office-of-civil-liberties-privacy-and-transparency).


The current **Principal Deputy Director of National Intelligence (PDDNI)** is **Dr. Stacey A. Dixon**, who was sworn in on August 4, 2021. [She serves as the sixth Senate-confirmed PDDNI and brings over 20 years of intelligence experience to the role](https://www.dni.gov/index.php/who-we-are/leadership/deputy-director-of-national-intelligence)[7](https://www.dni.gov/index.php/who-we-are/leadership/deputy-director-of-national-intelligence).

# Jennifer Ewbank to Step Down as CIA Deputy Director for Digital Innovation


[https://executivegov.com/author/jane-edwards/](https://executivegov.com/author/jane-edwards/)by [Jane Edwards](https://executivegov.com/author/jane-edwards/)


January 18, 2024


![Jennifer Ewbank to Step Down as CIA Deputy Director for Digital Innovation](https://em-graphics.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/documents/d4fa4db4-af7b-418b-8251-6a91954537c6.jpg)


Jennifer Ewbank / Central Intelligence Agency (CIA)


[Jennifer Ewbank](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jennifer-ewbank/), who has been serving in the federal government for more than three decades, will step down from her role as deputy director for digital innovation at the CIA.[https://events.executivemosaic.com/poc/topicspopup/poc-5th-annual-cio-summit/](https://events.executivemosaic.com/poc/topicspopup/poc-5th-annual-cio-summit/)


In a [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jennifer-ewbank_digitalinnovation-digitaltransformation-artificialintelligence-activity-7153496994303414272-Qrh_?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop) announcing the move, Ewbank said she will transition to the private sector, where she intends to continue her mission of strengthening U.S. national security.


In her current role at the CIA, she leads efforts to advance the development and integration of cyber and digital capabilities across all mission areas, including cybersecurity, enterprise information technology, open source collection and reporting, data strategy and artificial intelligence.


Ewbank, a two-time [Wash100](https://www.wash100.com/profile/2532) awardee, also oversees the offices of the agency’s digital chief information officer, chief information security officer and chief data officer.


She began her career at the State Department as a foreign service officer before joining the CIA.
