A Policy Paper by John Smith


Why the US Needs a Larger Military Buildup to Defuse the Crisis in the Middle East


# Introduction


The Middle East is a region of vital strategic importance for the United States and its allies. It is home to some of the world’s largest oil reserves, key trade routes, and regional partners that share common interests and values. However, the Middle East is also a region of instability, conflict, and violence, where Iran and its proxies pose a serious threat to the US and its allies. Iran has also been involved in several attacks on US and allied targets, such as the 2019 drone and missile strikes on Saudi oil facilities, the 2020 rocket attacks on US bases in Iraq, and the 2021 attack on the US embassy in Baghdad.


The US has responded to these provocations with a policy of maximum pressure, which includes economic sanctions, diplomatic isolation, and limited military strikes. However, this policy has not been effective in deterring Iran from its malign activities, nor has it induced Iran to return to the negotiating table for a comprehensive deal that addresses its nuclear and regional ambitions. On the contrary, Iran has continued to escalate its actions, while also reducing its compliance with the 2015 nuclear deal, known as the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA).


This paper argues that the US needs a larger military buildup in the Middle East to defuse the crisis and achieve its strategic objectives. A larger military buildup would enhance the US deterrence posture, limit Iran’s first strike capabilities, and force Iran to alter its strategic timetable. A larger military buildup would also enable the US to reconsolidate its regional position, reassure its allies, and create favorable conditions for diplomacy. A larger military buildup would not lead to an immediate de-escalation, but it would increase the costs and risks for Iran to continue its current course of action, and make it more likely to seek a negotiated solution in the long run.

# Why a Larger Military Buildup is Needed


The US needs a larger military buildup in the Middle East for three main reasons: to deter Iran and its proxies from further attacks, to reduce Iran’s first strike capabilities, and to force Iran to alter its strategic timetable.


- Deterrence: The US needs to increase its military resources in the region to signal its resolve and credibility to Iran and its proxies. The current US force posture in the region is insufficient to deter Iran from conducting asymmetric attacks on US and allied targets, such as drones, missiles, rockets, and mines. The US also lacks adequate air defense assets to protect its bases and partners from Iranian aerial threats. The US needs to deploy more troops, aircraft, ships, and missiles in the region, as well as more air defense systems, such as Patriot batteries and THAAD units, to Saudi Arabia and Bahrain. The US also needs to maintain a continuous presence of at least one aircraft carrier in the region, and preferably two, to project power and provide air cover. A larger military buildup would demonstrate the US commitment and capability to defend its interests and allies, and to retaliate against any Iranian aggression.
- First Strike Capabilities: The US needs to reduce Iran’s ability to launch a surprise attack on US and allied targets, especially in the event of a nuclear breakout. Iran has been developing and deploying a range of ballistic and cruise missiles, as well as drones and submarines, that could pose a significant threat to US and allied forces, bases, and infrastructure in the region. Iran has also been hiding and hardening some of its nuclear and missile facilities, making them more difficult to detect and destroy. A larger military buildup would reduce Iran’s confidence and window of opportunity to launch a preemptive or coercive attack on the US and its allies.
- Strategic Timetable: The US needs to force Iran to rethink its strategic calculus and timetable, and to increase the pressure for a diplomatic solution. Iran has been exploiting the perceived US weakness and withdrawal from the region, as well as the divisions among the US allies, to advance its nuclear and regional agenda. Iran has also been playing for time, hoping that the US will eventually return to the JCPOA without any additional concessions, or that the US will be distracted by other crises, such as China, Russia, or North Korea. The US needs to reverse this dynamic, and to make Iran realize that its current course of action is unsustainable and counterproductive. A larger military buildup would create a sense of urgency and uncertainty for Iran, and would make it more difficult for Iran to achieve its objectives without facing serious consequences. A larger military buildup would also create more leverage and incentives for the US and its allies to engage Iran in meaningful negotiations, and to reach a comprehensive deal that addresses not only Iran’s nuclear program, but also its regional behavior and missile program.
