The relationship between Saudi Arabia and Al-Qaeda is complex. Historically, individuals within Saudi Arabia have been accused of supporting Al-Qaeda, but the Saudi government has been a partner of the United States in counterterrorism efforts. [The Saudi government has also faced criticism for promoting a climate of radicalization through its support of religious preachers and organizations](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-u-s-saudi-arabia-counterterrorism-relationship/)[1](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-u-s-saudi-arabia-counterterrorism-relationship/)[2](https://www.factcheck.org/2015/02/saudi-arabia-and-the-911-terrorist-attacks/).


Wahhabism, a conservative movement within Sunni Islam, has had a global influence on Islamic practice. Saudi Arabia has funded religious activities and Islamic causes worldwide, promoting Wahhabi ideology. [This has affected Muslim communities on almost every continent, influencing theological discussions, religious scholarship, and debates on modernization within the Muslim world](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-u-s-saudi-arabia-counterterrorism-relationship/).


Examples of Shia Islamist groups include:


**Hezbollah**: A Lebanese political and militant group.


**Asa’ib Ahl al-Haq**: An Iraqi Shia paramilitary group.


[**Kata’ib Hezbollah**: Another Iraqi Shia militia. These groups have been influenced by the Iranian Revolution and the concept of Wilayat al-Faqih, which advocates for a theocratic governance system led by a qualified Shia jurist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamist_Shi%27ism).[https://www.factcheck.org/2015/02/saudi-arabia-and-the-911-terrorist-attacks/](https://www.factcheck.org/2015/02/saudi-arabia-and-the-911-terrorist-attacks/)
