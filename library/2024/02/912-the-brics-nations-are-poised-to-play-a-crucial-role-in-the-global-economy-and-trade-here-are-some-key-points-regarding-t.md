- [**New Development Bank (NDB)**: The NDB aims to strengthen financial cooperation and development projects within BRICS and other emerging economies](https://www.stimson.org/2023/the-future-of-brics-between-objectives-and-challenges/).
- The New Development Bank (NDB), initially known as the BRICS Development Bank, is a multilateral development bank established by the BRICS nations—Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa. The bank’s primary goal is to support public and private projects through various financial instruments such as loans, guarantees, and equity participation. [It focuses on funding infrastructure and sustainable development projects within BRICS countries and other emerging economies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Development_Bank)[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Development_Bank).
- The NDB also collaborates with international organizations and other financial entities to provide technical assistance for projects it supports. [With an initial authorized capital of $100 billion, the NDB aims to contribute to the development objectives of its member countries in alignment with their commitments under the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and the Paris Agreement](https://www.ndb.int/)[2](https://www.ndb.int/)[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Development_Bank).
- [In summary, the NDB is dedicated to financing projects that build a more inclusive, resilient, and sustainable future, with a focus on clean energy, transport infrastructure, water and sanitation, environmental protection, social infrastructure, and digital infrastructure](https://www.ndb.int/)[2](https://www.ndb.int/).

- The **Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)** are a collection of 17 interlinked global goals designed to be a “blueprint to achieve a better and more sustainable future for all” by 2030. [They were set up in 2015 by the United Nations General Assembly and are intended to be achieved by the year 2030](https://sdgs.un.org/goals)[1](https://sdgs.un.org/goals)[2](https://www.undp.org/sustainable-development-goals)[3](https://www.un.org/sustainabledevelopment/sustainable-development-goals/)[4](https://www.who.int/data/gho/data/themes/sustainable-development-goals).
- The **New Development Bank (NDB)** differs from other multilateral development banks in several ways:
- [**Equal Voting Rights**: Each member country has one vote, and no member has veto power, which is unique compared to other banks where voting power is often proportional to capital share](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Development_Bank)[5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Development_Bank).
- [**Focus on Emerging Economies**: While other banks may have a broader global focus, the NDB primarily supports public or private projects within BRICS and other emerging economies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Development_Bank)[5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Development_Bank).
- [**Lack of an Independent Accountability Mechanism**: Unlike most multilateral development banks, the NDB doesn’t have an independent accountability mechanism](https://theconversation.com/how-brics-new-development-bank-can-improve-transparency-and-accountability-186265)

According to the [US Energy Information Administration](https://www.eia.gov/tools/faqs/faq.php?id=709&t=6), the BRICS+ now includes six of the top ten oil producers in the world — Saudi Arabia, Russia, Brazil, UAE, Iran, and China — which together account for around 30 percent of global oil output. Moreover, China, India, Russia, Saudi Arabia, and Brazil are also top energy consumers. BRICS’ expansion will have implications for energy trade, as the BRICS will enable these countries to influence energy markets by fostering the harmonization of their interests, and potentially creating a united negotiation front on the world stage. But given the BRICS’ objective of promoting sustainable development and addressing climate change, it will be important to monitor how the inclusion of additional oil-producing countries will impact this agenda. As South African President Cyril Ramaphosa asserted during the August BRICS Summit, “BRICS is an equal partnership of countries that have differing views but a shared vision for a better world.” Despite concerns that the expansion of the BRICS may hamper the group’s cohesiveness, especially as it will make it more difficult for states to forge consensus, the group is uniquely positioned to support change if it succeeds in leveraging existing opportunities.

South Asia Regional Connectivity: Initiatives like the Bangladesh-Bhutan-India-Nepal (BBIN) and the trilateral highway project connecting India with Vietnam (currently facing challenges in Myanmar).The recent conflicts in the Red Sea have posed significant challenges to the India-Middle East-Europe
