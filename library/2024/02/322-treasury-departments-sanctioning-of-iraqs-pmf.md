The Popular Mobilization Forces (PMF), also known as Al-Hashd Al-Shaabi, is an Iraqi state-sponsored umbrella organization composed of various armed factions. While it is formally under the command of the Iraqi prime minister, it has significant ties to Iran and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC). [These ties have led to concerns about the PMF’s influence on Iraq’s sovereignty and its role in regional conflicts](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-popular-mobilization-force-is-turning-iraq-into-an-iranian-client-state/)[1](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-popular-mobilization-force-is-turning-iraq-into-an-iranian-client-state/)[2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Mobilization_Forces).


The PMF was formed in response to the ISIS threat in 2014 and has since become a key player in Iraq’s security landscape. However, its alignment with Iran-aligned factions has raised questions about its autonomy and the potential for it to act in Iran’s interests, particularly against U.S. [forces and allies in the region](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-popular-mobilization-force-is-turning-iraq-into-an-iranian-client-state/)[3](https://www.usnews.com/news/world/articles/2024-01-18/explainer-iran-and-its-proxies-and-widening-violence-in-the-middle-east)[4](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/new-vulnerabilities-for-iraqs-resilient-popular-mobilization-forces/).


[The PMF’s relationship with Iran and the IRGC underscores the complex dynamics of power and influence in Iraq, where state and non-state actors often intersect, and foreign interests can play a significant role in domestic affairs](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-popular-mobilization-force-is-turning-iraq-into-an-iranian-client-state/)[2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Mobilization_Forces).


In summary, the PMF is a paramilitary force in Iraq with strong ties to Iran and the IRGC, which impacts its operations and the broader security context in the Middle East.
