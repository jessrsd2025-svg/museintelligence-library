The event on October 6, 1973, referred to as the Yom Kippur War, was initiated by Egypt and Syria as a surprise attack against Israel. Here’s a brief summary:


**Yom Kippur**, most [solemn](https://www.britannica.com/dictionary/solemn) of [Jewish religious holidays](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Jewish-festivals), observed on the 10th day of the lunar month of Tishri (in the course of September and October), when [Jews](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Jew-people) seek to expiate their sins and achieve reconciliation with God. Yom Kippur concludes the “10 days of repentance”


**Background**: After losing the Sinai Peninsula, Gaza Strip, West Bank, and Golan Heights in the Six-Day War of 1967, Arab nations sought to regain territory. [Egyptian President Anwar el-Sadat planned a coordinated attack with Syria to alter the status quo and push for peace on more favorable terms](https://www.history.com/topics/middle-east/yom-kippur-war).


- **The Attack**: On Yom Kippur, the holiest day in the Jewish calendar, Egyptian and Syrian forces launched a coordinated assault. [Egypt attacked across the Suez Canal into the Sinai Peninsula, while Syria targeted the Golan Heights](https://www.history.com/topics/middle-east/yom-kippur-war).
- **The Response**: Israel, taken by surprise, suffered initial setbacks but eventually mobilized and repelled the invasions. With the aid of a U.S. [arms airlift, Israel regained lost territories and a cease-fire was established on October 25, 1973](https://www.history.com/topics/middle-east/yom-kippur-war).
- [**Aftermath**: The war had significant geopolitical consequences, including the start of peace negotiations between Egypt and Israel, which eventually led to the Camp David Accords](https://www.history.com/topics/middle-east/yom-kippur-war)


This conflict is a pivotal moment in Middle Eastern history, illustrating the complex interplay of military strategy, geopolitics, and the quest for peace in the region.
