# USCENTCOM Conducts Strike Killing Kata’ib Hezbollah Senior Leader


**USCENTCOM**


February 07, 2024
Release Number 20240207-01
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE


**TAMPA, Fla.** – At 9:30 p.m. (Baghdad time) February 7, U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) forces conducted a unilateral strike in Iraq in response to the attacks on U.S. service members, killing a Kata’ib Hezbollah commander responsible for directly planning and participating in attacks on U.S. forces in the region.


![](https://jessrsdincyahoocom.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/instability-in-iraq.png?w=853)


There are no indications of collateral damage or civilian casualties at this time. The United States will continue to take necessary action to protect our people. We will not hesitate to hold responsible all those who threaten our forces’ safety.


What is the Popular Mobilization Forces? Why did the US strike in Iraq and Syria? Who are some of Iran’s allies in the region?


The Popular Mobilization Forces (PMF), also known as Al-Hashd Al-Shaabi, is an Iraqi state-sponsored umbrella organization composed of various armed factions, predominantly Shia Muslim groups, but also including Sunni Muslim, Christian, and Yazidi groups. [Formed in 2014, the PMF has fought in major battles against ISIL and includes factions with ideologies ranging from Shia Islamism to anti-Americanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Mobilization_Forces)[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Mobilization_Forces).


The US strikes in Iraq and Syria were in response to a drone attack in Jordan that killed three American soldiers. [The US targeted facilities associated with the PMF and Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), aiming to degrade their capabilities and prevent further attacks](https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/world/us-strikes-targets-in-iraq-and-syria-after-middle-east-drone-attack-kills-three-soldiers/ar-BB1hGR8P)[2](https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/world/us-strikes-targets-in-iraq-and-syria-after-middle-east-drone-attack-kills-three-soldiers/ar-BB1hGR8P)[3](https://apnews.com/article/iraq-syria-strikes-hamas-militia-iran-us-62be7c7b500de1d0f1287a9b2c6f82dc).


Iran’s allies in the region include state actors like Syria and non-state actors such as Hezbollah. [These allies have been involved in various conflicts and have supported Iran’s regional objectives, often opposing US interests](https://www.csmonitor.com/text_edition/World/Middle-East/2024/0206/US-confronts-Axis-Who-are-Iran-s-allies-Can-they-be-deterred)[4](https://www.csmonitor.com/text_edition/World/Middle-East/2024/0206/US-confronts-Axis-Who-are-Iran-s-allies-Can-they-be-deterred)[5](https://www.cfr.org/article/irans-regional-armed-network).

The selected text refers to the potential risks of an escalation between the U.S. and Iran, particularly in the context of the ongoing conflict in Gaza. The Crisis Group experts mentioned—Brian Finucane, Lahib Higel, Naysan Rafati, and Ali Vaez—have discussed the implications of such an escalation. [They warn that the already devastating toll of the Gaza war could worsen if it leads to broader conflict involving the U.S., Iran, and their respective allies in the Middle East](https://www.crisisgroup.org/middle-east-north-africa/gulf-and-arabian-peninsula/iran-united-states/understanding-risks-us-iran)[1](https://www.crisisgroup.org/middle-east-north-africa/gulf-and-arabian-peninsula/iran-united-states/understanding-risks-us-iran).


The situation is complex, with various incidents contributing to the tensions. For instance, there have been attacks involving drones and indirect fire on U.S. forces based in Iraq and Syria, with Iran-backed groups in Iraq claiming responsibility. These attacks occurred amid a build-up of U.S. [military assets in the region as Israel continued its offensive in the Gaza Strip](https://www.crisisgroup.org/middle-east-north-africa/gulf-and-arabian-peninsula/iran-united-states/understanding-risks-us-iran)[1](https://www.crisisgroup.org/middle-east-north-africa/gulf-and-arabian-peninsula/iran-united-states/understanding-risks-us-iran).

[The strikes were a response to Houthi attacks on military and commercial vessels in the Red Sea, and the Houthis have stated that their attacks will not cease until Israel’s assault on Gaza ends](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2024/02/04/israel-hamas-war-news-gaza-palestine/)[2](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2024/02/04/israel-hamas-war-news-gaza-palestine/).

Liwa al-Tafuf, also known as the 13th brigade of the Popular Mobilization Forces (PMF), is a militia group operating primarily in Iraq and Syria. It has been designated as a terrorist organization by some entities due to its involvement in armed conflict and alleged ties to extremist activities.


Liwa al-Tafuf, the 13th brigade of the Popular Mobilization Forces (PMF), is indeed a militia group active in Iraq and Syria. It’s part of the PMF, an Iraqi state-sponsored umbrella organization composed of various armed factions. Liwa al-Tafuf is particularly noted for its logistical and administrative support to military operations against U.S. [forces in the region](https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/policy-analysis/profile-liwa-al-tafuf-13th-pmf-brigade)[1](https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/policy-analysis/profile-liwa-al-tafuf-13th-pmf-brigade). The brigade has been involved in significant battles against the Islamic State and has a history of association with Kataib Hezbollah, a group known for facilitating anti-U.S. [attacks](https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/policy-analysis/profile-liwa-al-tafuf-13th-pmf-brigade)[1](https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/policy-analysis/profile-liwa-al-tafuf-13th-pmf-brigade).


The PMF itself is a complex entity with a diverse composition, including Shia, Sunni, Christian, and Yazidi groups, and has been a key player in the fight against ISIL. [However, many of its main militias have origins tracing back to groups that previously fought against the United States and Coalition forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Mobilization_Forces)[2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Mobilization_Forces).


In summary, Liwa al-Tafuf is recognized for its role in regional conflicts and its connections to broader militia activities within the PMF framework. It has garnered attention and designation as a terrorist organization by some due to its actions and affiliations.
