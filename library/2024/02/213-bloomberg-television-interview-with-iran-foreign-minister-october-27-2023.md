In a recent interview with Bloomberg, Iran’s Foreign Minister Hossein Amir Abdollahian reiterated his country’s support for the Syrian government and its readiness to send more troops if needed. He also warned Israel that a ground invasion of Syria would have grave consequences for the region and its own security.


Amirabdollahian, who was in New York for the UN General Assembly, said that Iran has been providing military and humanitarian assistance to Syria since the start of the civil war in 2011. He said that Iran respects Syria’s sovereignty and territorial integrity and does not seek to impose its will on the Syrian people.


He also said that Iran is open to dialogue with other regional and international actors to find a political solution to the Syrian crisis, but he stressed that any such solution must respect the will of the Syrian people and their legitimate government.


However, he also expressed his concern about the increasing threats from Israel, which has repeatedly bombed Iranian and Syrian targets in Syria. He said that Israel is playing with fire and risking a wider war in the region. He warned that if Israel launches a ground invasion of Syria, Iran will not stand idly by and will respond with full force.


He said that Iran does not want war, but it will defend its interests and allies in the region. He urged Israel to respect international law and refrain from further aggression. He also called on the US and other Western countries to stop supporting Israel’s violations and to pressure it to end its occupation of Palestinian lands.


He said that Iran believes that peace and stability in the region can only be achieved through dialogue and cooperation, not through violence and intimidation. He said that Iran is ready to work with all countries that share this vision and respect its rights.

# Israel says it has struck more than 50 Hezbollah targets in Syria since Oct 7


Story by Reuters • 1d [https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/other/israel-says-it-has-struck-more-than-50-hezbollah-targets-in-syria-since-oct-7/ar-BB1hIVgS?ocid=socialshare](https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/other/israel-says-it-has-struck-more-than-50-hezbollah-targets-in-syria-since-oct-7/ar-BB1hIVgS?ocid=socialshare)


Israeli soldiers take part in training session near the Israel border with Syria at the Israeli-occupied Golan Heights, February 1, 2024. REUTERS/Gil Eliyahu/File Photo© Thomson Reuters


JERUSALEM (Reuters) -The Israeli military said on Saturday that since the outbreak of the Gaza war on Oct. 7 it had struck more than 50 targets in Syria linked to the Iranian-backed Lebanese movement Hezbollah.


The remarks, in a briefing by chief military spokesperson Rear Admiral Daniel Hagari that mainly discussed efforts to beat back Hezbollah attacks launched in solidarity with Hamas, were a departure from Israel’s usual reticence about Syria operations.


![](https://jessrsdincyahoocom.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/syria-linked-to-the-iranian-backed-lebanese-movement-hezbollah.jpeg?w=612)


“Everywhere Hezbollah is, we shall be. We will take action everywhere required in the Middle East,” Hagari said.


Israeli forces have attacked 34,000 Hezbollah targets in Lebanon, including 120 border surveillance outposts, 40 caches of missiles and other weaponry and more than 40 command centres, Hagari said. He put the number of enemy dead at more than 200.


Hagari said Israel had deployed three army divisions along its side of the Lebanese border in anticipation of Hezbollah getting involved after Palestinian Hamas launched a shock cross-border attack on Oct. 7, triggering the war in the Gaza Strip.


With tens of thousands of its northern residents having evacuated, Israel has threatened to escalate the Lebanon fighting unless Hezbollah backs off from the border – and has sought Western help in finding a diplomatic solution in Beirut.

**Militia Spotlight: Profiles** is a series by **The Washington Institute** that provides concise profiles for major Iraqi militia entities. These profiles offer basic data, including the entity’s name, logo, and type of activity. They also summarize the best available evidence regarding the entity’s history, alignment, and relationships with other groups. The goal is to assist journalists and government analysts in understanding the connections between these armed groups. [The profiles are regularly updated and serve as a repository of institutional memory on various armed groups associated with Iraqi militias](https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/policy-analysis/series/militia-spotlight-profiles)[1](https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/policy-analysis/series/militia-spotlight-profiles).


Additionally, **How to Use Militia Spotlight: Profiles** offers short, quickly digestible profiles that provide background information on militia-related organizations. [Each profile answers three key questions: the name of the entity, its English-language meaning, its logo, and a description of its nature and activities](https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/policy-analysis/how-use-militia-spotlight-profiles)[2](https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/policy-analysis/how-use-militia-spotlight-profiles).


[In summary, **Militia Spotlight: Profiles** provides valuable insights into the major Iraqi militia entities, aiding in understanding their affiliations and track records](https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/policy-analysis/series/militia-spotlight-profiles)
