The dynamics between Saudi Arabia, Iran, and the Houthis are central to understanding the conflict in Yemen and its broader regional implications. The Houthis, a Yemeni group aligned with Iran, have been engaged in an intense irregular warfare campaign against Saudi Arabia, using sophisticated missiles and drones. [This has led to numerous attacks on Saudi civilian targets and infrastructure, prompting Saudi military responses](https://www.csis.org/analysis/iranian-and-houthi-war-against-saudi-arabia).


[Iran’s support for the Houthis is part of a larger strategy to extend its influence across the Middle East, often referred to as the “Axis of Resistance.” This includes providing weapons, technology, and training to the Houthis, which has significantly enhanced their military capabilities](https://www.csis.org/analysis/iranian-and-houthi-war-against-saudi-arabia). The relationship between Iran and the Houthis, while not as deeply rooted as Iran’s ties with groups like Hezbollah, is nonetheless strategic, with both parties sharing opposition to Saudi Arabia and the U.S. [presence in the region](https://www.mei.edu/publications/houthis-iran-and-tensions-red-sea).

# Israel-Hamas War: Regional Escalation and U.S. Strategy


Monday, February 12, 2024


“As Saudi Arabia attempted to negotiate a permanent ceasefire with the Houthis, a new wave of violence erupted following Hamas’ attack on Israel. Abdulmalik al-Houthi, the Houthi leader, expressed solidarity with Hamas, aligning himself with the ‘axis of resistance.’ He threatened military action, particularly if the US intervened in support of Israel, accusing Saudi Arabia of betraying the Palestinian cause and serving as a US puppet. It’s noteworthy that Iran’s support for the Houthis began in 2017, about two years into the Saudi attack, possibly due to the Houthis’ ability to trouble the Saudis. The relationship between Iran and the Houthis is seen as opportunistic rather than central to Iran’s axis of resistance. The extent to which Iran can influence the Houthis, such as in pressuring them to negotiate or intercepting shipments, remains unclear.”
