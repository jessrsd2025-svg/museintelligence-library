The **Proof of the Truthful**[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_of_the_Truthful#cite_note-FOOTNOTEInati201428-1) ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): برهان الصديقين, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *burhān al-ṣiddīqīn*,[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_of_the_Truthful#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdamson2016126-2) also translated **Demonstration of the Truthful**[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_of_the_Truthful#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdamson2016126-2) or **Proof of the Veracious**,[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_of_the_Truthful#cite_note-FOOTNOTERizvi2009-3) among others) is a [formal argument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argument) for proving the [existence of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existence_of_God) introduced by the [Islamic philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_philosophy) [Avicenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avicenna) (also known as Ibn Sina, 980–1037). Avicenna argued that there must be a “necessary existent” (Arabic: واجب الوجود, romanized: *wājib al-wujūd*), an entity that cannot *not* exist.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_of_the_Truthful#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdamson2013170-4) The argument says that the entire set of [contingent things](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contingency_(philosophy)>) must have a cause that is not contingent because otherwise it would be included in the set. Furthermore, through a series of arguments, he derived that the necessary existent must have attributes that he identified with [God in Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_in_Islam), including unity, simplicity, immateriality, intellect, power, generosity, and goodness.
