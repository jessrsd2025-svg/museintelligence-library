Algorithmic Bias: Highlights how AI systems can produce biased results due to unrepresentative training data or hidden patterns of human discrimination.
Examples of Bias: Provides instances where AI models have shown biased outputs, such as associating certain races or genders with negative traits.

AI bias can manifest in various ways, often reflecting and amplifying existing human biases. Here are some notable examples:
Facial Recognition: Many facial recognition systems have been found to have higher error rates for people with darker skin tones and women1.
Hiring Algorithms: Amazon’s hiring algorithm was found to be biased against women, as it favored resumes that included more male-dominated terms.
Healthcare: Some healthcare algorithms have been shown to under-predict the health needs of Black patients compared to white patients with similar health profiles.
To mitigate algorithmic bias, several strategies can be employed:
Diverse and Representative Data: Ensuring that training data is diverse and representative of all groups can help reduce bias.
Bias-Aware Algorithms: Developing algorithms that are specifically designed to detect and mitigate bias during the training process4.
Continuous Monitoring and Evaluation: Regularly auditing AI systems for bias and making necessary adjustments can help maintain fairness4.
Ethical Considerations: Incorporating ethical guidelines and frameworks into the development and deployment of AI systems.
Regarding global standards, there isn’t a single unified standard for addressing AI discrimination, but several organizations and countries are developing guidelines and regulations. For example, the European Union has proposed the AI Act, which aims to regulate AI systems to ensure they are safe and respect fundamental rights5. Similarly, the IEEE has developed the “Ethically Aligned Design” guidelines to promote ethical AI development.
