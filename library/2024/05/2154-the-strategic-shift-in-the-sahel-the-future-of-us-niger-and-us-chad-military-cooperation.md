The Sahel region of Africa has long been a focal point for international security efforts, with Niger and Chad being pivotal partners in the fight against terrorism. However, the recent shift to military rule in both nations has cast a shadow over their ongoing cooperation with the United States, raising questions about the future of regional security dynamics.


Niger, which has served as a critical base for U.S. counterterrorism operations, has seen a significant change in its relationship with the U.S. following the junta’s decision to terminate an agreement allowing American troops to operate within its borders. This move necessitates a reevaluation of the U.S. military presence in the country and has led to discussions between the two nations on an orderly withdrawal of U.S. forces.


Chad, on the other hand, is undergoing a period of political transition with the long-awaited presidential election set to conclude years of military rule. The outcome of this election could redefine Chad’s military cooperation with the U.S., which has been a cornerstone of the American strategy in the region. The U.S. has expressed a desire to maintain a partnership with Chad, emphasizing the mutual benefits of security collaboration.


The U.S. investments in the region, including the $110 million drone base in Agadez, Niger, underscore the importance of these partnerships for surveillance and intelligence efforts across the Sahel. The potential withdrawal of U.S. troops from Niger and Chad poses strategic challenges, not only for the U.S. but also for the stability and security of the broader region.


As the U.S. reassesses its military footprint in the Sahel, the decisions made in the coming months will have far-reaching implications. The delicate balance between respecting national sovereignty and pursuing counterterrorism objectives will be at the forefront of diplomatic engagements. The evolving situation underscores the need for a nuanced approach that supports regional stability while adapting to the changing political landscape.


The international community will be closely monitoring the developments in Niger and Chad, as the outcomes will undoubtedly influence the strategic calculus in the Sahel and beyond. The hope is for a resolution that ensures continued collaboration in the fight against terrorism, while fostering a climate of trust and mutual respect between the nations involved.

Tensions Rise as Russian Military Personnel Enter Airbase in Niger


In a move that has escalated tensions in the Sahel region, Russian military personnel have entered an airbase in Niger that also houses US troops. This development comes amid a backdrop of shifting alliances and increasing geopolitical rivalry, particularly in the context of the ongoing conflict in Ukraine.


The presence of Russian forces at Airbase 101, which is adjacent to Diori Hamani International Airport in Niamey, has been confirmed by US Secretary of Defense Lloyd Austin. While the Russians are reportedly stationed in a separate hangar and do not have access to US forces or equipment, the proximity of the two nations’ troops is a cause for concern given the current strained relations between Russia and the United States.


This situation arises following the decision by Niger’s military junta to expel US troops from the country, a move that reflects a broader trend of Western forces being asked to leave African nations. The US, which had nearly 1,000 military personnel stationed in Niger, has been a key player in the fight against ISIL (ISIS) and al-Qaeda affiliates in the Sahel region. The withdrawal of US troops could have significant implications for the stability and security of the area.


The strategic implications of this development are profound. The US has invested heavily in the region, including a $110 million drone base in Agadez, which plays a crucial role in surveillance operations across the Sahel. The entry of Russian forces into Niger, coupled with the departure of US troops, could signal a shift in the balance of power and influence in the region.


As the situation unfolds, it is clear that the actions of Niger’s ruling junta and the subsequent deployment of Russian military personnel have the potential to alter the dynamics of international cooperation and security in the Sahel. The international community will be watching closely as these events continue to develop, with the hope that stability and peace can be maintained in the region.

### Humanitarian Assistance Efforts in the Sahel


- **Humanitarian Consequences**: Armed conflicts, financial limitations, and logistical constraints make delivering aid particularly difficult in the Sahel.
- **Legal Framework**: While the rules of international humanitarian law (IHL) on humanitarian access are clear, implementing them at the national level can be challenging. [States must balance security concerns with ensuring access to essential goods and services for affected populations](https://international-review.icrc.org/articles/challenges-to-implementation-humanitarian-access-norms-in-the-sahel-918)[1](https://international-review.icrc.org/articles/challenges-to-implementation-humanitarian-access-norms-in-the-sahel-918).
- **Measures Taken by Sahelian States**:
  - Declaration of states of emergency and armed escorts have been used to protect civilians and humanitarian organizations but can hinder impartial assistance delivery.
  - [Creative solutions include coordination mechanisms, domestic legal frameworks, and humanitarian exemptions in counterterrorism laws](https://international-review.icrc.org/articles/challenges-to-implementation-humanitarian-access-norms-in-the-sahel-918)[1](https://international-review.icrc.org/articles/challenges-to-implementation-humanitarian-access-norms-in-the-sahel-918).


In summary, the Sahel faces complex challenges related to civilian displacement, SSR, and humanitarian assistance. [Addressing these issues requires a delicate balance between security imperatives and meeting the essential needs of affected populations](https://www.dcaf.ch/sites/default/files/publications/documents/Mali%20transition_SSR%20opportunities%20and%20challenges%20v5.pdf)
