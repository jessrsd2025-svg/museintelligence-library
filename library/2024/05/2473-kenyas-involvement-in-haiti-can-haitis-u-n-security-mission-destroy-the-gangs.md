The situation in Haiti has been a growing concern for the international community, with gang violence reaching alarming levels and the state’s ability to maintain order severely compromised. In a significant move, Kenya has stepped forward to lead a United Nations Security Mission, aiming to assist Haitian police in securing key infrastructure and combating the criminal groups that have taken control of much of the country’s capital.


This mission, authorized by the United Nations Security Council in October 2023, represents a historic step not only for Kenya but also for the global community’s approach to peacekeeping and security assistance. It is the first time an African nation has led a security mission outside the continent, marking a new chapter in international solidarity and cooperation.


The Kenyan-led mission is expected to deploy soon, with the mandate to support the Haitian police in their fight against the gangs that are responsible for widespread abuses, including kidnappings, murders, and assaults. The mission faces significant challenges, not least of which are the legal and funding hurdles that have arisen. A Kenyan High Court decision found the order to deploy police officers to Haiti unconstitutional, although an appeal is pending, and the mission’s trust fund has received only a fraction of the estimated initial operational costs.


Despite these challenges, the need for international support in Haiti is urgent. The Haitian National Police are overwhelmed, and the military is modestly equipped, leaving a vacuum that gangs have been quick to fill. The UN has reported that in the first three months of 2024 alone, 2,500 people were killed or injured as a result of gang violence, and almost half of the victims were hit by bullets during violent attacks or clashes between gangs and the police.


The Kenyan-led mission is backed by the United States, which has become its loudest supporter and the mission’s biggest funder, despite choosing not to contribute forces. This support is crucial, as the mission aims not only to restore security but also to uphold human rights standards. The US government has confirmed it is vetting all Multinational Security Support (MSS) personnel as required by law, and the Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR) is collaborating on the design of the mission’s regulatory framework.


The effectiveness of the U.N Security Mission against the gangs in Haiti will depend on various factors, including the successful deployment of the mission, the cooperation of the Haitian government and police, and the support of the international community. The mission’s success will also hinge on its ability to operate with transparency and respect for human rights, ensuring that the intervention does not repeat the failures and abuses of past international responses.


As the world watches, the hope is that this mission will provide the Haitian people with the respite they desperately need and pave the way for a more stable and secure future. The involvement of Kenya in this mission is a testament to the country’s commitment to global peace and security, and a reminder that international challenges require international solutions.

The Caribbean nation of Haiti is in the grip of rampant gang violence, that might as well be called an all-out war. On March 3rd 2024 criminal elements organized a massive jailbreak of over 4,500 prisoners who overran the capital. Various heavily armed factions now control approximately 80% of Port-au-Prince, shutting down the airport and forcing more than 360,000 people to flee the city and its surrounding areas.

However, the local Haitian police force is close to being reinforced by a seven nation UN security mission led by Kenya. Their goal is to establish free and fair elections without simply adding to the ongoing violence. We’ll see how this is now the latest UN mission in a decades long string of controversial foreign interventions. What historical factors have complicated the response?
