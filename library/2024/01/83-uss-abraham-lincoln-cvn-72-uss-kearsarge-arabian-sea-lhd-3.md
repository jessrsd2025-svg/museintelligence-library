**Abraham Lincoln CSG and Kearsarge ARG Conduct Joint Operations in U.S. 5th Fleet**


Abraham Lincoln Carrier Strike Group (ABECSG) and Kearsarge Amphibious Ready Group (KSGARG) conduct joint operations in the U.S. 5th Fleet area of operations. The ABECSG and KSGARG, with the 22nd Marine Expeditionary Unit, are prepared to respond to contingencies and to defend U.S. forces and interests in the region. (US Navy photo by Mass Communication Specialist Seaman Catie Coyle/Released)


Joint Operations: The Abraham Lincoln Carrier Strike Group and the Kearsarge Amphibious Ready Group conducted joint operations in the U.S. 5th Fleet area of operations in May 2023.


U.S. Fifth Fleet (C5F), an Echelon III command, supports all naval operations in the U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) area of responsibility (AOR). It encompasses about 7.5 million square miles and includes the Arabian Gulf, Red Sea, Gulf of Oman, and parts of the Indian Ocean. This expanse, comprised of 25 countries, includes Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, United Arab Emirates, Iran, Iraq, Pakistan, and Somalia.


The usual force of 20-plus ships, with about 1,000 people ashore and 15,000 afloat, consists of a Carrier Battle Group, Amphibious Ready Group, combat aircraft, and other support units and ships. Fifth Fleet exemplifies the Department of the Navy’s strategic concept “Forward… From the sea,” by providing the ability to respond immediately to any emerging crisis from peace-keeping and humanitarian missions to asserting necessary force in regional conflicts.


Training Exercises: The operations included air-to-air training, steaming in formation and maneuvering, establishing joint communications, close air support, and visit, board, search and seizure training1.


Interoperability and Readiness: The operations demonstrated the ability of the U.S. Navy to quickly mobilize assets and aggregate different platforms with a variety of operational capabilities, as well as the tactical fluency and partnership with the U.S. Marine Corps.


Strategic Interests: The operations were a response to credible threat indicators and a show of force to deter destabilizing actions in the region and protect U.S. forces and interests.

The joint operations involved multiple exercises and maneuvers, such as air defense, surface warfare, maritime security, amphibious assault, and crisis response. The purpose of these operations was to enhance interoperability, flexibility, and combat effectiveness of the U.S. naval forces in the region. The ABECSG and KSGARG are prepared to respond to any contingencies and to defend U.S. forces and interests in the region. The joint operations also demonstrated the U.S. commitment to regional stability and security, as well as its resolve to deter any potential threats.


One of the aspects of the joint operations that relates to the DoD Directive 3000.09 is the use of autonomous and semi-autonomous weapon systems by the U.S. naval forces. The Directive establishes policy and assigns responsibilities for developing and using autonomous and semi-autonomous functions in weapon systems, including armed platforms that are remotely operated or operated by onboard personnel. The Directive also defines lethal autonomous weapon systems (LAWS) as weapon systems that, once activated, can select and engage targets without further intervention by a human operator . The Directive requires that such systems will be designed to allow commanders and operators to exercise appropriate levels of human judgment over the use of force, and that they will be used in accordance with the law of war, applicable treaties, weapon system safety rules, and applicable rules of engagement. The Directive also requires that such systems will have demonstrated appropriate performance, capability, reliability, effectiveness, and suitability under realistic conditions. Furthermore, the Directive requires that the design, development, deployment, and use of systems incorporating AI capabilities is consistent with the DoD AI Ethical Principles and the DoD Responsible AI (RAI) Strategy and Implementation Pathway.


The U.S. naval forces in the 5th Fleet area of operations have used various autonomous and semi-autonomous weapon systems in accordance with the DoD Directive 3000.09. For example, they have used unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) for intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance, targeting, strike, electronic warfare, and communications relay missions . They have also used unmanned underwater vehicles (UUVs) for mine countermeasures, anti-submarine warfare, oceanography, and hydrography missions . They have also used unmanned surface vehicles (USVs) for maritime security, force protection, anti-piracy, and logistics missions . These systems have enhanced the operational capabilities, reach, and endurance of the U.S. naval forces in the region. They have also reduced the risk to human personnel and increased the efficiency and effectiveness of the missions. However, these systems have also posed some challenges and risks, such as cyber vulnerabilities, ethical dilemmas, legal uncertainties, operational complexities, and human-machine interactions . Therefore, the U.S. naval forces have ensured that they have followed the guidelines and procedures established by the DoD Directive 3000.09 to ensure responsible and lawful use of autonomous and semi-autonomous weapon systems in the region.
