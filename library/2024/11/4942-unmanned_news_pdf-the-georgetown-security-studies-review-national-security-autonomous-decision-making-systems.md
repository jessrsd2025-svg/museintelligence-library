The Georgetown Security Studies Review (GSSR) recently analyzed the White House’s AI National Security Memorandum, highlighting its significance in shaping U.S. national security policy. The memorandum focuses on three main areas: accelerating AI development, promoting the adoption of advanced AI capabilities for national security, and building an international consensus on AI governance2.


Key points from the GSSR analysis include:


- **AI Development**: The memorandum aims to protect U.S. AI research from exploitation by competitors and supports the development of next-generation AI technologies.
- **National Security Applications**: It emphasizes the use of AI to address national security needs while ensuring these applications follow the law, protect human rights, and advance democratic values.

**Presidential Policy Directive 20 (PPD-20)**, provides a framework for U.S. cybersecurity by establishing principles and processes. Signed by President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) in October 2012, this directive supersedes National Security Presidential Directive NSPD-38. Integrating cyber tools with those of national security,[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Policy_Directive_20#cite_note-epicPres-1) the directive complements [NSPD-54/Homeland Security Presidential Directive HSPD-23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_National_Cybersecurity_Initiative).


Classified and unreleased by the [National Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Agency) (NSA), NSPD-54 was authorized by George W. Bush.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Policy_Directive_20#cite_note-epicPres-1) It gives the U.S. government power to conduct surveillance[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Policy_Directive_20#cite_note-epicNSA-2) through [monitoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_monitoring).[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Policy_Directive_20#cite_note-epicPres-1)


Its existence was [made public](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_surveillance_disclosures_(2013%E2%80%93present)>) in June 2013 by former intelligence NSA [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IT_infrastructure) analyst [Edward Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden).


Because of private industry, and issues surrounding international and domestic law,[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Policy_Directive_20#cite_note-barn-3) public-private-partnership became the, “cornerstone of America’s cybersecurity strategy”.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Policy_Directive_20#cite_note-natStrat-4) Suggestions for the private sector were detailed in the declassified 2003,[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Policy_Directive_20#cite_note-scahill-5) [National Strategy to Secure Cyberspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Strategy_to_Secure_Cyberspace). Its companion document, National Security Presidential Directive (NSPD-38), was signed in secret by [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush) the following year.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Policy_Directive_20#cite_note-scahill-5)


Although the contents of NSPD 38 are still undisclosed,[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Policy_Directive_20#cite_note-epicPres-1) the U.S. military did not recognize cyberspace as a “theater of operations” until the [U.S. National Defense Strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Military_Strategy) of 2005.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Policy_Directive_20#cite_note-barn-3) The report declared that the, “ability to operate in and from the global commons-space, international waters and airspace, and cyberspace is important … to project power anywhere in the world from secure bases of operation.”[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Policy_Directive_20#cite_note-natRep-6) Three years later, [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush) formed the classified [Comprehensive National Cybersecurity Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_National_Cybersecurity_Initiative) (CNCI).


- **International Collaboration**: The memorandum seeks to create a stable, responsible, and rights-respecting multinational governance framework for AI2.


The GSSR also discusses the potential policy shifts indicated by the memorandum, such as improved AI governance and risk management frameworks, and the implications for global security and geopolitical stability2.
