The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) is a bureau of the United States Department of the Treasury. [Its primary role is to collect and analyze information about financial transactions in order to combat domestic and international money laundering, terrorist financing, and other financial crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Crimes_Enforcement_Network)[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Crimes_Enforcement_Network). [FinCEN operates both domestically and internationally, working with law enforcement agencies, the regulatory community, and the financial-services community to track suspicious activities and ensure the integrity of the financial system](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/f/fincen.asp)[2](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/f/fincen.asp).

FinCEN uses Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) to combat financial crimes by analyzing the reports and sharing relevant information with law enforcement agencies. SARs are filed by financial institutions when they detect suspicious activities that may indicate money laundering, terrorism financing, or other financial crimes. [FinCEN’s analysis of SARs supports investigations and enforcement actions](https://www.financestrategists.com/banking/financial-crimes-enforcement-network-fincen/)[1](https://www.financestrategists.com/banking/financial-crimes-enforcement-network-fincen/).


The FBI plays a significant role in investigating financial crimes. It works closely with various government and private organizations to investigate securities and commodities fraud. The Bureau’s financial crime investigations may include tackling a variety of financial frauds, such as bankruptcy scams, accounting frauds, and violations of national banking laws. [The FBI also focuses on high-dollar and high-impact financial fraud cases, working in collaboration with partners to address these complex crimes](https://www.fbi.gov/news/stories/investigating-financial-crime)[2](https://www.fbi.gov/news/stories/investigating-financial-crime)[3](https://www.fbi.gov/investigate/white-collar-crime).

### Definition and Purpose of SARs


An SAR is a document that financial institutions must file with the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) to report known or suspected violations of law or suspicious activity. Financial institutions are encouraged to file a SAR even when dollar thresholds are not met to prevent potential harm caused by financial crimes.

- What is the role of SARs in mortgage fraud investigations?
- How to report suspected mortgage fraud to the FBI?
- The FD-71 Complaint Forms
- **Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs)** play a crucial role in mortgage fraud investigations. [They are documents that financial institutions file with the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) to report known or suspected violations of law or suspicious activity related to financial transactions, including mortgage fraud](https://www.saferwatchapp.com/blog/understanding-suspicious-activity-report-sar/)[1](https://www.saferwatchapp.com/blog/understanding-suspicious-activity-report-sar/). [SARs help law enforcement agencies, including the FBI, identify and investigate potential mortgage fraud by providing a paper trail of suspicious activities](https://www.mortgagesanalyzed.com/gyan/docs/suspicious-activity-report/suspicious-activity-report)[2](https://www.mortgagesanalyzed.com/gyan/docs/suspicious-activity-report/suspicious-activity-report).
- How to report suspected mortgage fraud to the FBI, you can:
- Use the FBI’s online tip form at [tips.fbi.gov](https://tips.fbi.gov/).
- Contact your local FBI field office.
- [Call the FBI’s toll-free hotline at 1-800-CALLFBI (1-800-225-5324)](https://www.saferwatchapp.com/blog/understanding-suspicious-activity-report-sar/)[3](https://www.wikihow.com/Report-Mortgage-Fraud).
- **FD-71 Complaint Forms** are used by the FBI’s Atlanta Field Office (ATFO) as part of their process to identify mortgage fraud violators and patterns of fraudulent activity. [These forms, along with SARs, are searched and analyzed by Special Agents to extract pertinent information for investigations into mortgage fraud](https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/more-fbi-services-and-information/freedom-of-information-privacy-act/department-of-justice-fbi-privacy-impact-assessments/mortgage-fraud)[4](https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/more-fbi-services-and-information/freedom-of-information-privacy-act/department-of-justice-fbi-privacy-impact-assessments/mortgage-fraud). [The FD-71 forms contain personal identifying information and details about the suspected fraudulent activity, which are then entered into the Mortgage Fraud Database for further analysis](https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/more-fbi-services-and-information/freedom-of-information-privacy-act/department-of-justice-fbi-privacy-impact-assessments/mortgage-fraud)[4](https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/more-fbi-services-and-information/freedom-of-information-privacy-act/department-of-justice-fbi-privacy-impact-assessments/mortgage-fraud).
