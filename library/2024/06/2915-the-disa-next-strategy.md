### The DISA Next Strategy (2025-2029)


The Defense Information Systems Agency (DISA) has outlined its vision for transforming the Department of Defense’s (DOD) information network through the DISA Next Strategy, which spans fiscal years 2025 to 2029. Here are the key points of this strategy:


#### Simplifying the Network

**Global Common IT Environment:**
DISA aims to create a unified IT environment by consolidating combatant commands, defense agencies, and field activities. This consolidation will enhance communication and eliminate unnecessary duplications across various departments.


#### Enterprise Cloud Environment


**DOD Enterprise Cloud:**
DISA plans to develop a fully functional DOD enterprise cloud environment. This cloud will provide both active and self-managed cloud spaces, ensuring secure access and relevant tools for DOD customers.


#### Identity, Credential, and Access Management (ICAM)


**Integration with IT and Cloud:**
ICAM and Zero Trust capabilities will be integrated into the common IT and cloud environment to enhance security and access management.


#### Resilient and Secure Transport

A resilient and secure transport infrastructure will underpin all these initiatives, ensuring robust and secure communication channels.


The DISA Next Strategy aligns with the agency’s mission to deliver capacity and capability to warfighters while adapting to dynamic challenges.


### The Thunderdome Initiative


Thunderdome is an initiative by DISA aimed at implementing Zero Trust architecture across the DOD’s information network. Zero Trust is a security model that requires strict identity verification for every person and device trying to access resources on a private network, regardless of whether they are inside or outside the network perimeter.


#### Key Aspects of Thunderdome


**Zero Trust Framework:**
Implementing a security model that assumes breaches and verifies each request as though it originates from an open network.


**Enhanced Security Measures:**
Introducing multi-factor authentication, continuous monitoring, and micro-segmentation to minimize risks and protect sensitive data.


**Modernizing Security:**
Modernizing the DOD’s security posture to counter evolving cyber threats and enhance overall network security.

Strengthening National Security: The MITRE-DoD Collaboration on the FiGHT Framework

The collaboration between MITRE and the Department of Defense (DoD) represents a significant stride in enhancing national security, particularly in the realm of cybersecurity. This partnership has led to the creation of the FiGHT (5G Hierarchy of Threats) framework, a comprehensive model designed to protect the 5G ecosystem from adversarial threats.

The FiGHT framework is a testament to the power of collaboration between government entities and research organizations. It leverages the foundational principles of the MITRE ATT&CK® framework, which is a well-established knowledge base of cyber adversary behaviors. The FiGHT framework extends these principles specifically to address the unique challenges posed by the 5G environment.

One of the key aspects of this collaboration is the open-source nature of the FiGHT framework. By making it freely available, MITRE and the DoD have provided a valuable tool for organizations to assess the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of their 5G networks. This approach encourages widespread adoption and continuous improvement of the framework through community engagement.

The FiGHT framework serves multiple purposes. It helps stakeholders identify where cyber investments should be made to achieve the highest impact as they build, configure, and deploy secure and resilient 5G systems. It also enables organizations to conduct threat assessments, perform adversarial emulation, identify coverage gaps, and inform cyber investment planning.

The collaboration also highlights the DoD’s commitment to deploying secure and resilient 5G systems. With the support of an engaged 5G stakeholder community, the FiGHT framework is poised to become a pivotal tool in the DoD’s cybersecurity arsenal. The partnership between MITRE and the DoD demonstrates the potential of collaborative efforts to address complex security challenges in an increasingly interconnected world.

Furthermore, the FiGHT framework is derived from a structured understanding of 5G threats, which, despite 5G being the most secure cellular standard to date, still presents risks and vulnerabilities. This collaborative effort fills a critical gap for telecommunication providers, manufacturers, and cybersecurity researchers, offering a foundation for 5G security research that can be operationalized in various ways.

The MITRE-DoD collaboration on the FiGHT framework is a clear example of how public-private partnerships can lead to innovative solutions that enhance national security. It underscores the importance of combining expertise from different sectors to create tools that not only protect critical infrastructure but also contribute to the stability and well-being of nations in the face of evolving cyber threats.


In conclusion, the FiGHT framework is a critical tool for understanding and addressing the myriad of threats that 5G technology faces. By categorizing and detailing specific adversary behaviors, the framework empowers organizations to conduct thorough threat assessments, enable adversarial emulation, identify coverage gaps, and inform cyber investment planning. As the 5G landscape continues to grow, the FiGHT framework stands as a foundational resource for ensuring a secure and resilient 5G ecosystem.

### How DOD-365 Enhances Cybersecurity


DOD-365 is the Department of Defense’s implementation of the Microsoft 365 suite, tailored for defense-specific needs. It enhances cybersecurity through several key features:


#### Integrated Security


Built-in security features such as advanced threat protection, information protection, and compliance solutions to safeguard data and communications.


#### Zero Trust Principles


Adopting Zero Trust principles ensures that all users and devices are continuously verified and authenticated.


#### Secure Collaboration


Providing a secure platform for communication and collaboration among DOD personnel, enhancing productivity while maintaining high security standards.


#### Cloud Security


Utilizing Microsoft’s secure cloud infrastructure, which includes data encryption, security management, and compliance with DOD standards.


### DISA’s Role in Securing Communication


DISA plays a critical role in securing communications within the Department of Defense by:


#### Network Security


Ensuring that the DOD’s information networks are secure from cyber threats through continuous monitoring and advanced security protocols.


#### Secure Communication Platforms


Developing and maintaining secure communication platforms that facilitate safe and reliable communication among military personnel and defense agencies.


#### Cyber Defense Operations


Conducting cyber defense operations to protect DOD networks from malicious activities and cyber attacks.


#### Infrastructure Resilience


Building and maintaining resilient and secure infrastructure to support DOD operations globally, ensuring continuous and secure communication.


### Conclusion


The DISA Next Strategy (2025-2029) aims to transform the DOD’s information network through simplification, a robust enterprise cloud environment, integrated ICAM, and resilient transport infrastructure. The Thunderdome initiative and DOD-365 enhance cybersecurity by adopting Zero Trust principles and providing secure collaboration tools. DISA’s role in securing communication is vital for maintaining the integrity and security of the DOD’s information networks, ensuring safe and reliable communication channels for warfighters and defense agencies.


### Strategic Alignment, Partnerships, and Adaptability


Air Force Lt. Gen. Robert J. Skinner, DISA director and JFHQ-DODIN commander, emphasized the importance of strategic alignment, partnerships, and adaptability during his keynote at TechNet Cyber 2024. He highlighted the need for strong partnerships between the DOD and private industry to bolster and protect the DOD’s network against strategic adversaries, particularly China​​.


Skinner outlined the DISA Next Strategy’s focus on enabling DOD and warfighter lethality through resilient, survivable, and secure capabilities. He mentioned key programs like Thunderdome, DODNET, and the Defense Enterprise Office Solution’s DOD-365 as successful initiatives contributing to this strategy​​.
