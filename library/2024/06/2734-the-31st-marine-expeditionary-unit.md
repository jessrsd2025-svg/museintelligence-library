A **Marine expeditionary force** (**MEF**), formerly known as a **Marine amphibious force**, is the largest type of a [Marine air-ground task force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_air-ground_task_force). A MEF is the largest building block of [United States Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Corps) combat power.


Prioritizing Aid Distribution: The Strategy of the 31st Marine Expeditionary Unit


The 31st Marine Expeditionary Unit (MEU) is a highly responsive quick reaction force, capable of conducting humanitarian assistance and disaster relief operations across the globe. A critical aspect of their mission is the prioritization of aid distribution, which is a complex process influenced by various factors.


When a crisis occurs, the 31st MEU works closely with the Department of State and host nation governments to assess the situation and determine the most urgent needs. This collaboration ensures that the aid provided is aligned with the priorities of those most affected by the disaster.


The MEU employs a rapid response planning process to launch teams for quick action. This process involves non-commissioned officers (NCOs) who are trained to understand the key components of humanitarian missions and are responsible for overseeing the execution of aid distribution plans.


Aid prioritization typically focuses on providing essentials such as food, water, and medical supplies. The MEU is equipped to create potable water from seawater and distribute food and other necessities efficiently, even in challenging environments.


In addition to immediate needs, the MEU also considers the long-term impact of their aid. They aim to provide initial relief that acts as a “tourniquet,” offering support until other government or non-government organizations can arrive to provide sustained assistance.


The Forward Command Element (FCE) of the 31st MEU plays a pivotal role in spearheading foreign aid. Inserting into the U.S. Embassy, the FCE coordinates with the Department of State to articulate the MEU’s capabilities and plan the assistance accordingly. This ensures that the aid distribution is not only timely but also effective in reaching those in need.


The prioritization of aid distribution by the 31st MEU is a testament to their adaptability, preparedness, and commitment to serving as a force for good in times of crisis. Their efforts continue to provide hope and relief to those affected by disasters around the world.

The strategic risk calculator evaluates eleven primarily military options that the United States and NATO could take to strengthen Ukraine’s defenses. These options span humanitarian assistance, the transfer of military equipment, and even covert and overt military actions within the country. The assessment considers two critical criteria:


1. **Military Effectiveness**: This assesses the chance that an option helps prevent the collapse of the Ukrainian military. It ranges from no/virtually no military effect to significant military effect.
2. **Risk of Escalation**: This evaluates the chance that an option leads to a direct NATO-Russia armed conflict. [It ranges from no/virtually no risk of escalation to limited risk of escalation](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/content-series/the-big-story/what-are-the-risks-and-benefits-of-us-nato-military-options-in-ukraine-our-strategic-risk-calculator-has-answers/)[1](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/content-series/the-big-story/what-are-the-risks-and-benefits-of-us-nato-military-options-in-ukraine-our-strategic-risk-calculator-has-answers/).
