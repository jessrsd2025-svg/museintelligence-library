China’s deep involvement in financing, building, and operating the region’s port infrastructure and its presence around the Panama Canal may allow it to extract concessions from host governments for preferential access. China has also made efforts to erode U.S. security partnerships in the region and increase Latin American and Caribbean countries’ dependence on China. Nevertheless, the United States retains strong relationships and historical ties with countries throughout the region. Many Latin American and Caribbean governments and publics desire much deeper U.S. engagement and are seeking to guard against risks from their relationship with China. As Dr. Berg noted in his testimony to the Commission, people in Latin America and the Caribbean want to “try to shield their countries from some of the most corrosive elements of… engagement [with China].”261 Robust cultural, educational, and social ties between the United States and the region through diaspora communities and proximate geography grant the United States important advantages that China cannot replicate. U.S. partnerships with governments, regulators, and civil society will have a key role to play in strengthening the region’s economies and institutions, advancing U.S. interests, and limiting the negative consequences of deepening Chinese engagement

1. **Increased Diplomatic Engagement**: The U.S. has ramped up diplomatic efforts, including high-level visits by U.S. officials to LAC countries. The aim is to strengthen bilateral ties and reassure these countries of the U.S.’s commitment to the region.
2. **Foreign Aid and Investment**: The U.S. has increased its foreign aid and investment in the region to counterbalance China’s financial influence. Programs like USAID have been enhanced to support development projects in LAC countries.
3. **Strategic Partnerships**: The U.S. has been reinforcing its strategic alliances and partnerships in the region. This includes renewed focus on military cooperation and joint exercises to ensure regional security.
4. **Promotion of Democratic Values**: The U.S. promotes democracy and human rights in LAC as a counter to China’s model of governance. Initiatives aimed at strengthening democratic institutions and civil society in the region have been prioritized.
5. **Economic Initiatives**: The U.S. has launched economic initiatives such as the “Growth in the Americas” (America Crece) initiative, which focuses on energy and infrastructure projects to enhance economic ties with LAC countries.


### Confucius Institutes


Confucius Institutes are educational organizations funded by the Chinese government with the goal of promoting Chinese language and culture globally. Here are key aspects:


1. **Purpose**: The primary purpose is to teach Mandarin Chinese, promote Chinese culture, and facilitate cultural exchanges. They also aim to enhance mutual understanding between China and other countries.
2. **Locations**: Confucius Institutes are typically hosted within universities and colleges around the world, including in many LAC countries. They offer language courses, cultural events, and sometimes provide resources for China-related studies.
3. **Controversy**: Confucius Institutes have been controversial, particularly in Western countries, due to concerns about academic freedom and propaganda. Critics argue that these institutes serve as tools for Chinese soft power, promoting a positive image of China while potentially censoring critical viewpoints.
4. **Programs and Activities**: Besides language instruction, Confucius Institutes organize cultural activities such as Chinese New Year celebrations, calligraphy classes, and Chinese cooking demonstrations. They also offer scholarships for students to study in China.


### Knowledge of China Among LAC Population


Despite the various efforts by China to enhance its image and influence in Latin America and the Caribbean, general knowledge about China among the LAC population remains minimal. Factors contributing to this include:


1. **Language Barrier**: The lack of widespread Mandarin education limits deeper understanding and engagement with Chinese culture and policies.
2. **Media Influence**: Local media coverage of China is often limited, and when present, it might focus on high-profile events rather than providing in-depth analysis of Chinese society, politics, or history.
3. **Educational Gaps**: Educational curricula in many LAC countries do not extensively cover Asian, and specifically Chinese, history and contemporary issues, leading to a lack of comprehensive knowledge about China.


These dynamics illustrate the complex and multifaceted nature of China’s charm offensive in the region and the corresponding U.S. responses.
