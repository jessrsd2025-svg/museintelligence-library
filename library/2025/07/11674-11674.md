### A. Structure and Provisions of the HALT Fentanyl Act


- **Permanent Scheduling:** Amends 21 U.S.C. § 812(c), classifying all fentanyl-related substances as Schedule I (no accepted medical use, high abuse potential). Structural definitions are broad to capture cartel-driven chemical modifications [Section 2].
- **Presidential Emergency Powers:** Mandates annual reports to Congress; uses IEEPA to sanction foreign fentanyl traffickers and associated organizations.
- **Attorney General Authority:** Enables real-time updates/additions to the Schedule I list, closing loopholes for new street analogues.
- **Anti-Money Laundering:** New reporting mandates for FinCEN and restrictions on international wire transfers linked to fentanyl trafficking.
- **Research Pathways:** Streamlined Schedule I research registrations for federally-funded studies, increasing access but without research-driven “off-ramp” to reschedule analogues.


**References:**

### B. Enforcement Obligations and Interagency Mandates


- **DEA:** Prioritize international precursor investigations; lead field task forces in open-air market crackdowns. Integrate intelligence from local, HIDTA, FBI, CBP, and Treasury.
- **DOJ/FBI:** Pursue criminal cases and international sanctions, including darknet operations (JCODE, Operation SpecTor).
- **FinCEN/Treasury:** Oversee SARs; issue anti-money laundering special measures; blacklist DTO-linked foreign banks.
- **CBP:** Deploy advanced scanning, K-9, and intelligence-driven inspections at border entry points; coordinate with DEA and ICE-HSI.
- **Local Law Enforcement:** Expand task forces (HIDTA), leverage federal resources, enforce nuisance and regulatory remedies.

## II. Implications for Enforcement, Research, Sentencing, and Public Health


### A. Enforcement Power and Impact


- **Open-air Market Focus:** Federal obligation to intervene via surges (e.g., Portland, Philadelphia, San Francisco), supplementing local enforcement when open-air trafficking persists post-decriminalization.
- **Border & Financial Focus:** Target Southwest corridors; restrict DTO money flows; block property related to trafficking networks.
- **Escalated Sentencing:** Quantity-based mandatory minimums apply to fentanyl analogues; legal precedents affirm broad prosecution authority (McCray, Johnson).


### B. Impact on Research


- **Streamlined Schedule I registration** slightly lowers administrative barriers for medical research (Sec. 3) but retains chilling effects—no explicit mechanism for scientific/therapeutic analogues to be easily descheduled (“off-ramp”).
- **Critiques:** NIDA, civil rights coalitions warn this may stifle innovation and stymie development of pain, OUD, or antidote medicines.


### C. Public Health and Community Harm Reduction


- **Dual response:** Enforcement escalation (mandatory minimums, mass seizures, federal task forces) vs. harm reduction (naloxone/test strip distribution, prevention teams).
- **Resource allocation:** Elevated funds for DEA, CBP, DOJ, but also significant expansion in HIDTA funding for overdose investigations and youth prevention.
- **Sentiment and Communication:** “Plague” narrative sustains political and public urgency, but risks crowding out health-based, treatment-first messaging.

The **Drug Market Agency Coordination Center (DMACC)** operates as a multi-agency enforcement and intelligence hub—like in San Francisco’s Tenderloin District—but its legal authority draws from broader federal enforcement frameworks rather than a single named CFR section. Here’s how it maps out:


## 🏛️ Key CFR Sections Supporting DMACC Enforcement Authority


### 🔹 28 CFR Part 0 Subpart R – Drug Enforcement Administration


This subpart outlines DEA’s enforcement powers and coordination responsibilities:


- **§ 0.101(a)**: DEA develops and implements federal drug law enforcement programs and coordinates with state/local agencies.
- **§ 0.102**: DEA reports to the Attorney General and leads drug enforcement policy coordination.
- **§ 0.103(a)**: DEA can release investigative reports and authorize testimony in drug-related prosecutions.
- **Appendix to Subpart R**: Delegates enforcement powers (e.g., subpoena issuance, property seizure under 21 U.S.C. §§ 875–881) to DEA agents and task force members.


### 🔹 21 CFR Part 1300–1399 – Controlled Substances Regulations


These DEA regulations govern:


- **Part 1305 & 1311**: Electronic orders and digital certificate systems for Schedule I–II substances.
- **Part 1308**: Scheduling of controlled substances, including fentanyl analogues.
- **Part 1316**: Administrative inspections, subpoenas, and seizure procedures.


### 🔹 21 CFR Part 7 – FDA Enforcement Policy


While not directly tied to DMACC, this part governs recall and enforcement actions for violative products, including drug-related public health threats.


## 🧩 How DMACC Fits In


DMACC isn’t codified as a standalone federal entity in the CFR—it’s a **local implementation of federal enforcement powers**, combining:


- DEA task force authority under **28 CFR Part 0**.
- Controlled substance enforcement under **21 CFR Parts 1300–1399**.
- Local police powers and municipal ordinances.


It leverages federal scheduling, seizure, and intelligence-sharing statutes to coordinate arrests, seizures, and public health interventions in open-air drug markets.
