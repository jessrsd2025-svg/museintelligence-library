### IRGC Control of Iranian Oil Exports & China Linkages


- **IRGC Revenue Stake:** *Iran’s Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) controls up to 50% of the country’s oil exports,* a sharp rise since 2020. Oil allocated directly to IRGC (and its Quds Force) forms the backbone of funding for its domestic power and regional proxy operations.
- **Shadow Trade & Smuggling Networks:** To evade Western sanctions, the IRGC leverages a “shadow fleet” of tankers, opaque logistics, and a web of front companies. These entities facilitate clandestine shipping and discount oil deals—primarily with China.
- **China’s Centrality:** *China is the key buyer for sanctioned IRGC oil,* with transactions frequently involving Chinese-run front firms like Haokun Energy. These entities, sometimes run by former Chinese PLA officers, have remained active channels despite U.S. Treasury sanctions.
- **Discount Mechanism:** Oil moved by the IRGC is sold at a discount—reported between $1 and up to $8 per barrel—compared to state oil channels, compensating buyers for increased risk and enabling large-volume circumvention of sanctions.
- **Strategic Export Partnership with China:** Most IRGC-linked sales are to independent Chinese refineries via front companies. Chinese risk appetite (and willingness to ignore U.S. designations) underpins ongoing sales and IRGC revenue.
- **Financial Flows:** Revenue is split roughly evenly between IRGC and state oil trading firms (NICO). Funds then support IRGC operations, proxies like Hezbollah, and possibly regional destabilization.
- **Evasion Tactics:** The IRGC employs ship-to-ship transfers, vessel disguise, and reflagging tactics (hallmarks of the “ghost fleet”) to mask crude origin and frustrate international sanctions enforcement.
- **U.S. & Israeli Response:** Western sanctions have targeted not only Iranian oil entities, but also dozens of tankers tied to the Quds Force and affiliated networks. Enforcement efforts have intensified as IRGC’s share—and flexibility—in smuggling increases.
- **Oil as Power:** The Guards’ rise in oil oversight signals not just economic clout, but *strategic autonomy* from civilian state bodies (like NIOC). The process was built up under Qassem Soleimani’s tenure.
