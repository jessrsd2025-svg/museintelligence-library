**CRS reports on notification requirements or oversight frameworks**:[**Covert Action and Clandestine Activities of the
Intelligence Community]:**a **non-statutory but accepted practice** in U.S. intelligence oversight known as **Gang of Four notifications**. These are used when the executive branch deems an intelligence activity—typically **non-covert but highly sensitive**, such as a surveillance or collection program—too delicate to brief the full congressional intelligence committees.


Instead of notifying all members, the executive provides **oral briefings to just four individuals**: the chairs and ranking members of the House and Senate intelligence committees. This limited disclosure is not required by law and isn’t codified in committee rules, but it’s a **customary workaround** accepted by congressional leadership to balance secrecy with oversight.


The process is governed in part by **ICD-112**, a directive that outlines how intelligence activities (excluding covert actions) must be reported to Congress. While covert actions fall under **50 U.S.C. §3093**, ICD-112 ensures that even non-covert but impactful operations—like new surveillance technologies or sensitive foreign intelligence collection—are still reported, albeit sometimes in this restricted format.[https://www.csis.org/analysis/war-proxy-irans-growing-footprint-middle-east](https://www.csis.org/analysis/war-proxy-irans-growing-footprint-middle-east)
